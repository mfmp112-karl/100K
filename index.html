The situation
What we're working with and why it matters
The group
100k Leaks Lab
35 members. WhatsApp Community. Went quiet due to low engagement killing momentum. Members are existing business owners — not hobbyists — who are stuck selling only to people they already know.
The product
4-Week Biz Pawa Up
Karl coaches business owners personally over daily calls and WhatsApp for 4 weeks until they get customers they didn't already know. KSh 5,000 (group) to KSh 25,000 (1:1). Guarantee: first new-money sale in Month 1 or refund.

The core problem to solve: The group is quiet because nothing posted before felt directly relevant to what members were actually experiencing. The fix is not better content — it's better targeting. From this point, every post is written for one specific person: an existing business owner who is making some money but cannot figure out how to grow beyond the people who already know them.

Critical insight
The group doesn't know you don't know what they sell
There is no data on what these 35 people sell. This is the first problem to fix — before a single post goes out. The individual DM outreach in Phase 1 solves this. Everything after it becomes more targeted because of it.
01
Master sequence
The order of operations. Do not skip or reorder.

The sequence is: DMs first → re-entry post second → data series third → 4-week arc fourth. A quiet group cannot be reactivated by posting into it. It must be reactivated person by person first, then addressed as a group.

Phase 1 — DM all 35 individually
Days 1–3
Re-warm every member privately. Ask what they sell. Log every reply. This is intelligence gathering, not outreach. The replies become your content bible.
Phase 2 — Re-entry post
Day 3–4 (once 8+ DM replies are in)
First group message. Not a lesson — an announcement of new direction. Earns permission to teach. Sets expectation for what's coming.
Phase 3 — Data series (4 messages)
Week 1–2 (3x per week pace)
Four messages that expose the cost of operating without information. Teaches the importance of data using zero jargon and 100% Kenyan SME examples. Creates the felt need for everything that follows.
Phase 4 — 4-week content arc
Weeks 3–6
12 posts mirroring the Biz Pawa Up exactly. Diagnose → Build → Get customers → Lock it in. Coaching offer introduced naturally at the end of Week 4. No pitch before then.
Phase 5 — Private DM to most engaged members
Week 4 (alongside final posts)
Identify 2–3 members who replied most consistently. DM them directly with the 1:1 coaching offer before the group-wide mention. First paying clients should come from here.
02
DM outreach
Before the first group post. 35 individual messages.
Purpose
This is not marketing. It is reconnaissance. You need to know what these 35 people sell before you post anything to the group. The DM also re-warms a cold contact — they remember who Karl is before any group post hits their screen. Without this step, you are broadcasting into silence.

Hey [name] 👋 It's Karl from the 100k Leaks group. I've been rebuilding the whole direction of the group and I want to make sure what I share actually applies to what you're doing.

Quick one — what kind of business are you running right now? Even if it's still small, I want to know.

What to listen for
How many are product-based (food, fashion, crafts) vs service-based (cleaning, beauty, errands). Whether they mention a problem without being asked — that's a future post topic. Whether any say "I've been meaning to do something with this business." Those are your first coaching conversations.
Log every reply
Keep a simple note: Name / What they sell / Status (active, dormant, just starting). After 35 replies, you will know exactly which examples to use in every post. Reference real members in posts without naming them: "One member in this group sells cakes from her kitchen and told me she has no idea which flavour makes her the most money." That specificity is what gets replies.
Timing rule
Do not wait for all 35 to reply before posting to the group. Start DMs Monday. Post the re-entry message Wednesday or Thursday once you have at least 8–10 replies. The intel from replies shapes your future posts — not the first one.
03
The re-entry post
First group message. Not a lesson — permission to teach.

Why this is not a content post: A group that went quiet because of low engagement needs trust restored before information is delivered. If you open with a lesson, the people who never replied to anything will think "here we go again." The re-entry post is an announcement of new direction — it earns permission, it does not spend it.

This group has been quiet for a while. That's on me — I wasn't posting things that actually connected to what you're dealing with day to day.

That changes now.

Starting this week I'm going to be walking through the exact 4 things every small business owner needs to figure out to stop selling only to people they already know.

It's not complicated. But most people skip at least one of them — and that's exactly where the money stops.

First one drops [day]. See you then. 🔑

What this does
Takes accountability without over-apologising. Names the new direction clearly. Creates anticipation for what's coming without revealing it. Does not ask for engagement — states what's happening.
What to avoid
Do not open with a question. Do not ask "what do you want to learn?" Do not apologise excessively. Do not tease with "something big is coming." State the direction and post it.
04
The angle
The single lens everything is taught through.
"You are not broke.
You are operating blind."
This is the thread running through every post, every week, every data conversation. Your audience isn't failing because they're lazy or unskilled — they're failing because they don't know what's actually happening inside their business. No customer data. No revenue tracking. No pattern recognition. They are making decisions in the dark and wondering why nothing improves. Every post you write exposes one more thing they didn't know they didn't know.

This angle matters because your audience has heard "you need to market better" a hundred times and tuned it out. But "you don't know what's happening inside your own business" is different. It's not an accusation. It's a revelation. Most of them will read it and think: he's right, I actually don't. That moment of recognition is what makes them stay in the group.

06
The data series
4 messages. Sent before the 4-week arc begins.

Why data first: Your audience doesn't care about systems, frameworks, or coaching until they feel the pain of operating without information. These 4 messages create that feeling. Each one exposes a specific blind spot using a real Kenyan SME scenario. By Message 4, they are thinking: "I actually don't know my own numbers." That is the moment they become ready to learn anything you teach next. The data posts are also a slow-motion hand raise — every "NO" reply is someone telling you they need what you're selling, without you having pitched anything.

Message 1 The sale vs. the profit problem Pain point #02
Teach the difference between revenue and profit using one example a food seller or service provider would recognise instantly. End with a binary question they can answer in their head right now. Replies of "NO" are your leads.
Most small business owners count sales. The ones who grow count profit. There's a difference — and it's costing you money right now. Say you make 10 mandazis and sell them all for Ksh 500. You spent Ksh 320 on oil, flour, and gas. You made Ksh 180. Not Ksh 500. That gap between what came in and what it cost you — that's the number your business actually runs on. Most people never write it down. So they never see it shrinking. Quick one: Do you know your profit number from last week? Not sales — profit. Reply YES or NO. No judgment. 👇
Message 2 The customer you forgot about Pain point #04
Show what a dormant customer is worth vs. the cost of finding a new one. Make them count how many people they've sold to who never came back. End with a concrete homework that takes 2 minutes.
How many people bought from you in the last 3 months — and never came back? Don't guess. Actually think about it. Every one of those people already trusted you enough to give you money once. They're not strangers. They're the easiest sale you're not making. Getting a new customer costs effort — posts, outreach, convincing. Getting an old customer back costs one message. But most business owners don't even know who those people are. No name. No number. No record. That's not bad luck. That's missing data. Your homework: Write down 3 names of people who bought from you and went quiet. Just 3. We'll talk about what to do with that list next time. 📋
Message 3 The product you're pushing vs. the one making you money Pain point #03
Walk them through finding their most profitable product in 10 minutes using nothing but their memory. No tools required. Exposes the gap between what feels popular and what actually pays.
Quick question: which product or service makes you the most money? Not the one that sells the most. The one that leaves the most in your pocket per sale. Most business owners don't know. They push what's popular — which is usually the cheapest item with the thinnest margin. Here's how to check right now: Take your top 3 products. For each one, subtract what it costs you to deliver it from what you charge. That gap is your margin. Whichever gap is biggest — that's the product your business should be built around. If you've never done this, you've been guessing. And guessing keeps you stuck. 🔍
Message 4 The full picture — and what's coming Pain points #01 + #08
Tie the three previous messages together. Name the pattern — operating blind. Then introduce the 4-week arc as the solution without pitching it yet. Creates anticipation, not pressure.
Over the last few messages I've been asking you to look at three numbers: Your profit. Your dormant customers. Your best margin product. If you found it hard to answer any of those — that's the real problem. Not your marketing. Not your prices. Not the economy. You're running on memory and guesswork. And memory is not a business system. The businesses that grow consistently are not smarter. They just know their numbers. They know who bought last week, what worked, and what to do tomorrow. That's what I'm going to walk through here — step by step — starting next week. Four things. One per week. By the end you'll know exactly what's blocking your growth and what to do about it. Stay here. 🔑

The operating rules
Non-negotiable. These protect the momentum being built.
Post 3x per week, same days every week. Consistency is what rebuilds trust in a quiet group — not quality alone. Pick Monday, Wednesday, Friday and protect them.
Every post ends with either a question or a homework. The group is only alive if people respond. Responses require prompts. Never post without one.
Reply personally to every single person who responds. In a group of 35, individual replies are visible to everyone. Each personal reply signals to the whole group that engagement is rewarded.
Use real examples from the 35 DM replies in every post. "A member in this group told me they sell cakes and have no idea which flavour makes the most money" hits 10x harder than a generic scenario. No names — just details.
Never pitch the product before Week 4, Post 12. Every earlier mention of coaching should feel like a logical conclusion the reader reaches themselves — not a sales move from Karl.
Track engagement per post in a simple note. Date, post topic, number of replies, who replied. After 4 weeks you will know exactly which topics your audience cares about most. That data shapes Season 2.
If a week is missed, do not double-post to catch up. Missing one week resets the streak — it does not justify three posts on Monday. Acknowledge the gap in one line, reset, and continue the next scheduled post.
Keep every post under 200 words. WhatsApp is a thumb-scroll environment. Long posts get read by the sender and no one else. If it needs more than 200 words, it needs to be split into two posts on separate days.
The one thing to do today
Write the 35 DMs
Not one template — personalise each with whatever is remembered about that person. Even "hey, are you still doing the cakes?" shows you paid attention. That detail is the difference between a reply and silence. Start today. The group post can wait until Wednesday.
